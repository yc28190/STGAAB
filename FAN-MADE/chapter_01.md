# Chapter 1 — Abyss Door

Eight years.

That is how long I spent on *Dungeon and Stone*. In France we called it *Donjon & Stone*, because the first fan translation got the title half right and nobody ever bothered to fix it. Eight years, two keyboards, one broken engagement, and a spreadsheet with forty-one tabs.

My name is Eric Dupont. I'm thirty-two, I work in logistics in a warehouse outside Lyon, and I can tell you the spawn timer of every elite on the third floor faster than I can tell you my mother's birthday.

I'm not proud of that. I'm just being accurate.

The game has a reputation, and it earned it: permadeath, no tutorials worth the name, monsters that read your inputs, and a story that only reveals itself if you're willing to die a few thousand times to learn it. Most people quit in the first week. The rest of us stayed, swapped notes on dead forums at three in the morning, and slowly turned the labyrinth from a nightmare into something like a commute.

My main was always a dwarf tank. A heavy shield, a short hammer, and enough stubbornness to stand in front of things that should not be stood in front of. Barbarians hit harder and humans have better skills, but nothing in that game holds a corridor like a dwarf who refuses to move.

I could reach the tenth floor reliably. I want that on the record. Not once, not by luck. Reliably.

And every time, it ended the same way.

The tenth floor has a door at the bottom of the world. The community called it the Abyss Door. Everyone agreed it was the ending, and nobody knew how to open it. There's a sequence, a pattern you have to perform in front of it, and the game gives you nothing. No hint, no inscription, no NPC muttering in a corner. I spent two years testing theories. Item orders. Step counts. Standing still for an hour. Dying in front of it on purpose, in case that was the trick.

It wasn't.

So, three weeks ago, I did the thing I'd sworn I would never do.

I downloaded the cheat version.

Everybody knew about it. It had been floating around for years, passed from link to link: a modded build with a menu full of toggles. Infinite stamina. Visible traps. Essence drop rate multipliers. Most of the people who claimed to have finished the game had finished *that*, and the real players sneered at them.

I opened the menu and stared at it for a long time.

Then I ticked exactly one box. *Experience ×2.*

Nothing else. No god mode, no map reveal, no guaranteed drops. I told myself it wasn't cheating, just saving time. A man with a full-time job is allowed to save time.

And I'll admit something petty: it was the best run of my life.

Not because the game was easier. It wasn't, not really. It was because I finally had enough levels, at the right moments, to do everything properly. I didn't have to skip side areas to stay on schedule. I cleared every hidden piece I'd ever found. My dwarf walked into the tenth floor overgeared, overleveled and sober for the first time.

And the cheat version, it turned out, did something the original never did.

When I reached the Abyss Door, there was text on it.

Just one line, in the pixel font I knew better than my own handwriting. A sequence. Seven steps.

I laughed out loud at two in the morning, alone in my apartment, with the neighbours' dog barking through the wall. Seven steps. Two years of my life, and the answer had been carved on the door all along, just not in *my* version of the game.

I did the seven steps.

The door opened.

There was no cutscene and no music. The screen went white, slowly, from the edges in, like paper catching fire backwards. A message box appeared in the centre, plain grey, the kind the game used for system notices:

「Save data verified.」
「Modifications detected: 1.」
「Thank you for playing.」

Then the white reached the middle of the screen and kept going.

I remember thinking: *my monitor's dying.*

Then I couldn't feel my chair.

---

The first thing that came back was the smell. Coal smoke, hot iron and something sour, like old beer spilled on stone a long time ago.

The second was the weight.

My body was *heavy*. Not tired-heavy; dense, the way a sandbag is dense. My arms lay across my chest like logs. When I tried to lift my hand to my face, it came up slowly, and it was wide, scarred across the knuckles, with fingers like short thick sausages.

There was hair on my face. A lot of it. I could feel it against my neck when I turned my head.

I was lying on a narrow cot in a low room with a stone ceiling. Somewhere beyond the wall, a hammer was ringing on an anvil in a steady rhythm, *tink, tink, tink*. Three strikes and a pause. Three strikes and a pause. A good smith's rhythm.

I sat up. The cot creaked under me as if it had opinions about my weight.

On the wall opposite, someone had hung a round shield. Dented, cheap, iron-rimmed. A starter shield. I knew that shield. I had started a hundred and twelve runs with that shield.

My throat went dry.

"No," I said.

The voice that came out was not mine. It was deep, rough, and about an octave lower than it had any right to be.

The hammering outside stopped. Footsteps. The door scraped open, and an old dwarf with a braided grey beard tucked into his belt filled the frame, wiping his hands on a leather apron.

"Finally awake," he grumbled. "Twenty years old and you sleep like a stone. The checkpoint opens at dawn, boy. If you're not standing in line with the rest of the new ones, the city will tax you for the privilege of waiting."

Rafdonia, then. Of course it was Rafdonia. The last city. The only place left where people could live, and the only place that would gladly kill you for being late on your taxes.

I looked down at my hands again, at the hammer calluses I had never earned.

Eight years I had played this game.

And now, apparently, I was going to have to play it for real, in a body that wasn't mine, with one small modification on my record.

I really, truly hoped nobody here knew how to read save data.
