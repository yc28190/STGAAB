# Chapter 1 — Abyss Door

Eight years.

That is how long I spent on *Dungeon and Stone*. In France we call it *Donjon & Stone*, because the first fan translation got the title half right and nobody ever bothered to fix it. Eight years, two keyboards, one broken engagement, and a spreadsheet with forty-one tabs.

My name is Eric Dupont. I'm thirty-two, I work in logistics in a warehouse outside Orléans, and I can tell you the spawn timer of every elite on the third floor faster than I can tell you my mother's birthday.

I'm not proud of that. I'm just being accurate.

The game has a reputation, and it earned it: permadeath, no tutorial worth the name, monsters that read your inputs. Most people quit in the first week. The rest of us stayed, traded notes on half-dead forums at three in the morning, and slowly turned the labyrinth from a nightmare into something like a commute.

My main was always a dwarf tank. A heavy shield, an axe in the other hand, a spare axe on the back, and enough stubbornness to stand in front of things that should not be stood in front of.

I could reach the tenth floor reliably. I want that on the record.

And yet, in eight years, nobody had ever posted a clear. Not one screenshot. Not one forum thread that survived scrutiny.

We knew what was waiting at the end, more or less. The NPCs talked about it: the old men in the taverns, the priests, the mad sage in the second-floor ruins. *The Abyss Door.* The door at the bottom of the world. Everybody knew the name. Nobody had seen it.

Because the last stretch of the tenth floor is a corridor of traps, and most of them kill you instantly. Not "heavy damage". Not "survive with one HP if you're lucky". A tile clicks, and your character, your eight hundred hours, your run, is gone.

I died in that corridor thirty-seven times. I counted.

Three weeks ago, I gave up on my principles and downloaded the cheat version.

Everyone knew about it. A modded build with a menu full of toggles: infinite stamina, visible traps, essence drop multipliers, all of it. I opened the menu, stared at it for a long time, and ticked exactly one box.

*Experience ×2.*

Nothing else. It wasn't cheating, I told myself. Just saving time.

It was the best run of my life. With twice the experience, I hit every level threshold early. More levels meant more essence slots, and more slots meant my dwarf carried a stack of defensive essences no honest run could have afforded by that point. Damage reduction on damage reduction. Regeneration. A passive that turns lethal blows into survivable ones.

So when I stepped on the first instant-death tile of that corridor, for the thirty-eighth time…

I didn't die.

The trap still went off. My health bar collapsed to a red sliver. But the sliver stayed.

I sat there with my hand over my mouth for a full minute. Then I drank every potion I had and limped the rest of the way, one tile at a time.

And at the end of the corridor, there it was.

A door of black stone, taller than anything else in the game, carved with spirals that seemed to move when you weren't looking at them directly. The Abyss Door. Eight years of rumours, and it was right there, two pixels from my dwarf's nose.

I pressed the interact key, and my brain immediately started working.

*Final boss.* Obviously. Nothing in this game gives you a reward without making you bleed for it. So what was behind the door? The demon lord the priests kept warning about? Something that summoned every floor boss at once? Something with phases, surely, at least three. Maybe a phase that strips your essences, which would be exactly this game's sense of humour.

I checked my inventory. Two potions left. My shield at sixty percent durability. One axe chipped, the backup intact. Not great. If the boss opened with an area attack, I would have to tank it on the shield and pray. If it summoned adds, I'd fall back to the doorway and hold the chokepoint. A dwarf tank's whole life is holding chokepoints.

I wiped my palms on my jeans, put my fingers back on the keys, and leaned toward the monitor.

The door swung open.

There was no boss.

There was white. It spread from the edges of the screen inwards, slowly, and in the centre a plain grey box appeared, the kind the game used for system notices:

「Tutorial complete.」

I read it three times.

*Tutorial?*

Eight years. Ten floors. Thirty-seven deaths in a single corridor. *Tutorial?*

I was still trying to decide whether to laugh or throw my keyboard when the white reached the middle of the screen and kept going, out of the monitor, over my desk, over my hands.

Then I couldn't feel my chair.

---

The first thing that came back was the smell: coal smoke, hot iron, and something sour, like beer spilled on stone a long time ago.

The second was the weight.

My body was *heavy*, dense the way a sandbag is dense. When I lifted my hand to my face, it came up slowly, and it was wide, scarred across the knuckles, with fingers like short thick sausages. There was hair on my face. A lot of it.

I was lying on a narrow cot in a low room with a stone ceiling. Beyond the wall, a hammer rang on an anvil: three strikes and a pause, three strikes and a pause.

On the wall opposite hung a round iron shield, dented and cheap, and beside it a short, badly balanced hand axe. A starter set. I had started a hundred and twelve runs with that exact set.

"No," I said.

The voice that came out was deep and rough, an octave lower than mine.

The hammering stopped. The door scraped open, and an old dwarf with a grey beard braided into his belt filled the frame.

"Finally awake," he grumbled. "Twenty years old and you sleep like a rock. The checkpoint opens at dawn, boy. Be late, and Rafdonia will tax you for the privilege of waiting."

Rafdonia. The last city.

I looked at my hands again, and a horrible thought crept in. Nobody had ever posted a clear. Not because nobody had ever made it.

Because everyone who did ended up here.
