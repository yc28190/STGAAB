## Description
The barbarians' sacred homeland, a stretch of green forest enclosed by a towering wall with a single gate opening onto the edge of the 7th District; the city walls are visible in the distance and the sun sets beyond them. A forest path leads to a residential area that is more campsite than village: huts and tents pitched haphazardly with no sense of ownership, where even the chieftain sleeps in a tent. At the center lies a torch-lit clearing where young warriors train and where the tribe holds its meetings and announcements outdoors, standing, since it has no meeting halls. Beyond the camp the forest grows dense, wild and gloomy, including the Warrior's Forest and a forest used for funerals. In wartime a translucent barrier rose high above the wall. Later the Sanctuary was partly redeveloped with paved roads and new buildings, including one where the chief's tent used to stand; its festival is dominated by drums and percussion.

The Sanctuary serves as a communal nursery: barbarians living in the city send their children here to be raised, and they leave for the city after their coming-of-age ceremony. It holds the shaman's tent where Spirit Engravings are given, the chieftain's and elders' tents, and a graveyard. Although placed under Rafdonia, it actually lies outside the main city walls, hidden behind illusion magic, officially out of respect for non-humans but really as exclusion. Barbarians have lived in the city for about 600 years, and the territory's resources are exhausted after millennia of settlement.

## Mechanics
- Coming-of-age ceremony: the chieftain calls each youth as "X, son/daughter of Y"; each picks a melee weapon (no bows or staves) and is led to the city gate. Under Bjorn, new warriors also receive leather boots and two potions, shield-pickers a hammer, and Bonding teams for first expeditions (ch. 2–3, 127, 452–455).
- Anyone who utters taboo words such as "Dungeon and Stone" is executed on the spot as an evil spirit and the temple notified (ch. 2).
- Spirit Engraving (tattoo soul imprinting) is done by the blind shaman in his tent: Undying Engraving stage 1 cost 800,000 stones in one account and 550,000 in another (conflicting accounts), stage 2 1,000,000, stage 3 2,000,000. Every 3rd stage lets one choose a soul (Flame, Steel or Wildness); from stage 6, materials are needed (Bjorn paid 1 million plus a Heart of Immortality for the Soul of Armaments with [Unification]); stage 7 requires the "Soul of the Deceased" offering (ch. 21, 77, 79, 152, 392).
- The shaman also tells fortunes with sticks drawn from a jar and periodically undergoes "Soul Purification", during which he cannot be seen (ch. 77, 262).
- Soul Inheritance and "tempering" (6 months of seclusion) take place here (ch. 152).
- The chieftaincy is won by defeating the chieftain in a duel and can be challenged at any time; the winner gains the "Approval Rating" stat (ch. 247–248, 451–452).
- Other races are barred; humans need the chieftain's permission and a pass. City-born barbarians may lack access. The gate guard expects a password, but a barbarian war cry opens it (ch. 247, 391, 449, 453).
- Commoners cannot own land in the kingdom, but Sanctuary land can be bought with the chieftain's permission, which funds the Sanctuary Redevelopment Plan (ch. 452–453, 466, 494).
- The royal family grants a two-year tax exemption per birth; barbarian pregnancy lasts about 4 months (ch. 248).
- The forest is unsafe for hiding items, since graves are dug there for funerals (ch. 332, 334).
- An old royal magic circle can cut the Sanctuary off from the city completely, as happened during the Noark invasion, sparing it heavy damage (ch. 623, 662).
- An invisible wall at the Sanctuary's edge blocks the way into a forest beyond; the shaman said it was "not yet time" to wonder about it (ch. 662).
- The wartime barrier was powered by barbarians stationed inside; when its magic supply was cut it failed within about an hour (ch. 739, 741).

## Notable events
- ch. 2–3: Oreum, son of Kadua, is beheaded as an evil spirit; Bjorn's coming-of-age ceremony takes place the same day the Genesis Treasure is stolen.
- ch. 20–21, 77–79: Bjorn receives his first Undying Engravings; Ainar trains under a swordmaster elder.
- ch. 151–154: Bjorn gives a guest lecture; after he declares he will become chieftain, the chieftain (a former 8th-floor explorer) knocks him out, then accepts his ambition. The previous chieftain died in the Sacred Relic War with the fairies 10 years earlier.
- ch. 247–248: Bjorn spars with the chieftain for about an hour, losing four times.
- ch. 257–259: Karon and Bjorn swap faces with the Golden Mask here to fool the GM.
- ch. 286–287: In the past timeline Bjorn leaves an anonymous letter warning of the Genesis Artifact theft and the Sacred Relic War.
- ch. 295: The tribe closes its gates and holds a days-long funeral for Bjorn.
- ch. 450–455: Bjorn defeats Gudnulf Olga and becomes the youngest chieftain ever; he finds the treasury empty and reforms administration with Shabin Emure's human clerks, naming Ainar 1st Elder and Karon 2nd Elder.
- ch. 494–497: Bjorn's march on the Mage Tower starts here, followed by a huge victory party.
- ch. 633–651: The Sanctuary shelters 8,712 refugees; house-building contests turn warriors into a workforce, and the Yandel Trading Company is founded, the first company ever founded by barbarians.
- ch. 739–746: Refugee clans shelter here during the war. When the barrier fails, a Noark magic cannon destroys the gate; the defenders feign a retreat and collapse the wall at the gate to cut off the attackers. Bjorn saves Rotmiller, then leads out 207 explorers and 800 handpicked warriors.
- ch. 773–774: After the war the dead are brought back; warriors are buried in the forest with a memorial stone for the missing, and humans with headstones.
